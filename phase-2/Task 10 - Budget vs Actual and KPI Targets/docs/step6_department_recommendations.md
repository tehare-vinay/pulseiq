# Task 10 — Department Budget Recommendations

## 1. Objective

Recommend practical actions for the two departments with the highest positive payroll budget variance, based on 18 comparable months of budget and governed actual payroll cost.

## 2. Department D03 — Rank 1

- Total Budget: ₹10,97,32,000
- Total Actual: ₹11,07,24,768.20
- Positive Variance (Overspend): ₹9,92,768.20
- Variance Percentage: 0.90%

**Recommendations**

1. Review monthly payroll cost against the approved department budget to identify when overspending occurs.
2. Investigate changes in overtime, staffing levels, and payroll adjustments before approving additional payroll expenditure.
3. Set a monthly budget variance review with the department manager and Finance team.
4. Track the variance trend and take corrective action if overspending continues.

## 3. Department D02 — Rank 2

- Total Budget: ₹7,00,20,000
- Total Actual: ₹7,07,83,163.71
- Positive Variance (Overspend): ₹7,63,163.71
- Variance Percentage: 1.09%

**Recommendations**

1. Review monthly payroll expenses and identify the periods contributing most to the positive variance.
2. Monitor overtime and workforce changes against approved staffing plans.
3. Introduce an approval review for additional payroll expenditure that may exceed the allocated budget.
4. Compare actual payroll cost with budget every month and escalate recurring overspending.

## 4. Monitoring and Follow-up

- Review budget versus actual payroll cost monthly.
- Monitor overtime and workforce-related KPI traffic lights alongside payroll variance.
- Assign department managers responsibility for investigating and explaining variances.
- Reassess recommendations after the next monthly reporting cycle.

## 5. Conclusion

D03 has the highest absolute overspend, while D02 has the higher variance percentage. Both departments should strengthen monthly payroll monitoring and investigate the factors contributing to their positive budget variances.

*Note: These recommendations are based on aggregate department-level figures. They do not identify individual employees or establish the specific cause of overspending.*
